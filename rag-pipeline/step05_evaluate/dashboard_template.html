Our three methods side by side

All three use the same model, the same instructions and the same checks. Only what the model reads changes:

  • Hybrid search: we split the report into chunks, search them for the parts likely to hold results, and send the model the best chunks, up to about 12,000 tokens (roughly 9,000 words).
  • Full parsed text: we turn the whole PDF into text (keeping tables) and send all of it.
  • Full PDF: we send the PDF file itself and let the model read it.

Page checks and page repair

The model tells us which page each value is on. We don't take its word for it. After the model answers, our code opens the PDF and checks:

  • Is the number printed on that page? If yes, the row is marked "checked on page".
  • If not, is there exactly one page that has both the number and the quote? Then the code moves the row to that page. That is a page repair. The model's original page is kept in the row, so nothing is hidden.
  • Is the quote really in the PDF? If not, the row is flagged for review.
A page repair, full PDF, P010 The model said "Technologies generated: 175" is on page 34. Page 34 of the PDF doesn't have it. PDF page 39 does, and the top of PDF page 39 is printed "Page 34 of 68". The model used the page number printed on the paper, not the PDF's page. The code moved the row to page 39.

How often was the model's page right?

Full PDF's mistakes follow one pattern: in — of its — repairs the real page was exactly — pages later, which is the length of the cover pages before "Page 1". Hybrid and full parsed read text where we mark each page, so they almost never get it wrong.

Why are rows flagged for review?

Most flags are about the quote, not the number. For tables, the model often joins pieces that are far apart in the PDF into one quote, and our quote check can't find that joined text. The number itself was still found on the page in — of hybrid's — flagged rows.

A flagged row (hybrid, P001) New knowledge and advocacy products disseminated at country level · 12.00 · page 34 · number found on page: yes quote: "New knowledge and advocacy products disseminated at country level ... Actual Achieved at Completion | 12.00 15-Jun-2018" flag: Quote not found in the PDF The label, the column header and the value are all on page 34, but not next to each other, so the joined quote isn't found word for word.

Full PDF is flagged far more often (— of rows) because most of its quotes are not word for word in the PDF: it tends to reword or stitch them together instead of copying the text. The current pipeline's protocol says it also checks each value against its cited page, but the saved files don't include those check results, so we can't compare.

Strengths and weaknesses

Current pipeline (R)

Strengths
Found about as many answer-key results as we did (— of —).
Fills the Alliance's whole template, not just results: project details, budget, actors and locations, mapped to the Alliance's code lists. Its location sheet has one row per place with a location code, which ours does not have.
Pulls out more numbers in total, for example the whole yield table in P006.
Weaknesses
Many results are not in proper columns. Of the — answer-key results it found, — are not in its 3 headline slots: they are in the free-text notes cell (one is also in the location sheet). Example: P010's 4,612,946 beneficiaries, 175 technologies, 40,472 lead farmers and 37% female are all inside one sentence.
Its 3 headline slots use short labels from the Alliance's metric list ("CANDIDATE:" when none fits), and one is wrong. Example: P010's 95% is labelled "smallholder farmers reached"; the report says it is lead farmers aware of a technology.
Leaves yes/no results out on purpose, so it can't record milestones like P001's "knowledge platform up and running: N".
Was developed on these same reports, so this is its best case.

Our pipeline (all three methods)

Strengths
Every result is its own row with value, indicator, page and quote, and baseline, target and year when the report gives them. Example: "Direct project beneficiaries · 4612946 · page 37 · baseline 0 · target 6,100,000 · year 2020".
Every page is checked against the PDF by code, and wrong pages are repaired (section 5).
Keeps yes/no milestones, labelled as milestones.
Weaknesses
Pulls out results only. It does not yet fill the rest of the Alliance template or map anything to its code lists (actors, locations, metric names).
Skipped numbers R kept, such as P006's control-plot yields.
Missed the same narrative result R missed. Example: P001's "more than 1,000 African participants in e-learning events" is in a paragraph on page 17; no output has it.
Many quotes are flagged because they join table pieces (section 5).

What this tells us

  • On finding results, the two pipelines are about even on these 3 reports: — for R and — to — for ours, out of —.
  • The real difference is the shape of the output. Ours puts each result in its own row with a checked page and a quote. R's are often inside a notes sentence (see the 4,612,946 example in section 1).
  • Both missed the one result that is only in a paragraph, not a table (P001's e-learning participants). It's one case, but it's the kind of thing to watch on more reports.
  • The answer key has problems of its own: 2 of its 16 rows are flagged by the key itself, and nobody found them.
  • Hybrid search is the best value of our methods: close to the best on results, the cheapest, and almost always on the right page.

What we can't claim yet

  • That we're better at finding results. One or two results either way on 16 is noise.
  • How many of our values are right. We checked the 16 answer-key results, not our other 200 or so rows. "Number found on its page" only means the number is printed there, not that it's the right indicator. Same for R's extra values.
  • How we'd do on new reports. These are 3 of the 10 reports R was developed on. The 10 holdout reports (H001 to H010) are the fair test.
  • A same-model comparison. R's saved output came from a different model (gpt-5-mini, per its protocol) and different prompts.
  • Anything about the rest of the template. R's 91% accuracy figure is about project fields like budget and years, which this page doesn't test.

Next

  • Hand-check a random sample of our other rows, and R's, to see how many are right.
